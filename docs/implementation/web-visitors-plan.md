# Web visitors implementation plan

Drafted November 4, 2026 from the fingerprinting research summarized below. Not yet shipped. This is the first slice of roadmap application 21 (website and product analytics). It gives a workspace retroactive attribution: an anonymous browser session on the business's website becomes attached to a Contact's timeline once that person identifies themselves through a form, booking, concierge conversation or tokenized email link.

## Integration decisions

The supplied plan is the design baseline. Fingerprinting is optional per site and disabled by default; cookie-only operation remains useful. Consent is required in the first release. The host must proxy collection on its own origin for a first-party HttpOnly cookie; calling `suite.loo.ski` from another site does not create a first-party website cookie. Browser restrictions still apply to server-set cookies; no retention or recognition guarantee follows from a 400-day expiry.

A fingerprint match creates a reversible scored link, never a merged visitor, verified identity, permission or reusable concierge session. Preserve separate visitors and show uncertain attribution explicitly. Neither an exact hash nor a 0.9 similarity score implies a 90% probability of identity. Calibrate on observed drift and collisions before enabling automatic suggestions; no automatic person-level attribution by default.

Captured form/booking/concierge email is a declared identity, not proof of mailbox control. Signed email clicks may be forwarded or generated by scanners: record them as attribution candidates, not deterministic resolution. Verification requires a separate challenge or an authenticated session. Probabilistic history must never enter an external concierge response as though it were authenticated customer history. Resolution and manual linking use existing Contacts authority and record evidence/actor/source.

Retain only needed components, bound request size and candidate scans, strip page query strings/fragments and referrer paths, and honor withdrawal with a collection stop and cookie clearing. Resolved data also needs an explicit retention policy; contact association is not permission for indefinite retention. Exported pseudonymous hashes remain sensitive; no third-party sharing is enabled by this feature.

Implement the Postgres foundation before adding ClickHouse. Page-view delivery may be lossy only when visibly documented; identity links, consent, deletion jobs and contact evidence are durable. Deletion completion must verify all enabled stores. The fallback matcher cannot be a way around withheld or withdrawn consent.

## Concierge boundary

Keep the concierge conversation cookie and the visitor attribution cookie independent. A returning fingerprint never selects or restores a previous conversation session. The browser collector runs only after a separate recognition opt-in, not merely because someone submits the existing request-for-response contact form. Declining collection leaves chat, forms and booking fully usable.

The authenticated concierge bridge may attach a visitor evidence ID to a newly captured lead or booking. The staff inbox can show suggested earlier visits with recipe version, evidence, match score and an explicit uncertain label. Staff with independent Contacts and visitor permissions may confirm or reject the association; that action is audited and reversible. The external assistant receives only the current conversation's declared facts and verified results. It never receives a fingerprint candidate's contact details, old messages, appointment codes or identity token.

Ship matching as an operator-controlled capability disabled by default, with exact and similarity modes separately selectable. No UI may describe a hash match as a verified person. Acceptance must include two unrelated browsers with identical fingerprints: both sessions stay distinct and neither receives the other's data. Withdrawing recognition consent stops capture and clears the first-party visitor cookie; a later visit cannot silently recover the withdrawn association through fingerprint fallback.

## Research conclusions that shape the design

- There is no standard. Every library hashes the same bag of browser signals (canvas, WebGL/GPU, audio, fonts, screen, timezone, languages, voices). The commercial tiers add server-side signals (IP, headers, TLS handshake) and evolution-aware matching. The two maintained MIT libraries are ThumbmarkJS 1.12.0 and FingerprintJS 5.3.0; both state that the free client-side hash is coarse, with heavy collisions on Mac/Safari.
- Fingerprints drift. FP-STALKER (IEEE S&P 2018) measured changes every few hours to days from browser and OS updates, and could still link a browser for about 54 days on average using per-attribute rules. Brave and Firefox strict mode randomize canvas per session. Chrome does not.
- A fingerprint never identifies a person. Identity arrives through a first-party deterministic event. Person-level "visitor identification" vendors match their own pixel's cookies against purchased identity graphs keyed on hashed email; that is proprietary data the suite excludes.
- Avoiding cookies does not avoid consent. EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 (final, October 2024) place fingerprinting under the same ePrivacy Article 5(3) rule as cookies. The fingerprint is a resilience measure against cleared cookies and Safari's script-cookie cap, not a consent exemption.
- The only portable identifiers across outside directories and ad platforms are SHA-256 of the lowercased email, hashed E.164 phone, and IP plus timestamp plus user agent. Raw fingerprint components are useful only internally, for re-matching after drift and re-hashing after a library upgrade.

## Scope

In scope: a visitor record per workspace, a server-set first-party cookie, fingerprint component capture, probabilistic re-linking when the cookie is absent, page-view events, deterministic resolution to a Contact, Contact timeline composition, consent gating, retention, export and permanent deletion.

Explicit exclusions: third-party identity graphs, IP-to-company datasets, bot and VPN scoring, cross-site tracking, dashboards and funnels (later analytics slices), and any automatic write to Contact fields from a probabilistic link. Outside enrichment, if a workspace wants it, is a future connected application that exports hashed emails; it is not a suite capability.

## Components

### Browser snippet (`@looski/visitor`, npm, MIT)

A thin ESM/UMD package in `apps/visitor` published separately from the AGPL server. It wraps `@thumbmarkjs/thumbmarkjs` with `logging: false` and `collect_beacon: false`, collects the component map, and posts `{components, page, referrer}` to the workspace's public collection endpoint. It never computes identity, never reads or writes storage itself, and runs only after the host page reports consent. The hash is computed server-side so the recipe can change without a client release. Target under 20 KB gzipped including the dependency.

### Collection endpoint (Rust, `crates/looski/src/visitors`)

`POST /api/v1/public/visitors/{site_key}/events` with origin allowlist and throttling matching the public form and helpdesk endpoints. On each call the server:

1. Reads the `looski_visitor` cookie (HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, Secure, bounded expiry, subject to browser storage restrictions even when server-set). A valid cookie resolves the visitor directly.
2. Otherwise computes the component hash and attempts an exact hash match against the workspace's visitors seen in the last 60 days.
3. Otherwise scores similarity against unlinked visitors from the same window (see matching) and links above the configured threshold, recording the score.
4. Otherwise creates a new visitor, sets the cookie, and stores the components.
5. Appends a page-view event keyed by visitor ID.

The request IP and user agent are stored on the visitor row for the current session only and are never written to ClickHouse. The server adds `Accept-Language` and user-agent client hints to the component map.

### Matching

Exact match on the recipe hash first. The fallback is a weighted similarity score in [0, 1] over components, not a hash and not an embedding, because most components are opaque hashes with no usable geometry.

- High weight, rarely changes: platform, GPU renderer and vendor, screen geometry and pixel ratio, timezone, hardware concurrency, canvas hash on the same GPU, audio hash.
- Low weight, drifts with updates: browser version, font list (Jaccard over sets), plugins, speech voices (Jaccard), math precision.
- Hard rules that veto a match: platform differs, browser family differs, browser major version decreases.

Default threshold 0.9, configurable per workspace. Every link records its score and reason so thresholds can be tuned. Visits that carry a valid cookie but produce a different hash are stored as confirmed drift pairs; they are the free training set if a learned scorer is added later. Candidate retrieval is a bounded scan of recent unlinked visitors, which is adequate at on-premises traffic levels; MinHash candidate retrieval is deferred until a workspace exceeds roughly 100,000 visitors in the window.

### Resolution to a Contact

The following events supply attribution evidence in the same browser session; apply the verification distinctions above before resolving a Contact:

- a public form submission or booking whose request carries the visitor cookie;
- a concierge session whose lead email is captured, passed through the existing bridge with the visitor ID;
- a click on a signed link in Suite outbound email, which proposes an attribution candidate without binding an identity.

Resolution calls the existing Contacts intake path and records a `visitor_resolved` activity. It does not create or edit Contact fields on its own. Once resolved, the SHA-256 of the lowercased email and hashed phone are stored on the visitor row as the portable keys for any future export. Earlier visitors linked by probabilistic match remain separate attribution candidates and are shown to authorized staff with their link score; they do not inherit verified identity.

### Storage

Postgres (`visitors` schema, one migration) holds personal data and is the authority:

- `visitors.visitor` — id, workspace, site, cookie digest, current hash, first/last seen, resolved contact, resolution event, hashed email, hashed phone, current-session IP and user agent, retention deadline.
- `visitors.components` — one JSONB map per visitor per recipe version, the raw material for re-hashing and re-matching.
- `visitors.links` — probabilistic merges between visitors with score and reason, reversible.
- `visitors.site` — per-workspace site key, allowed origins, consent mode, threshold, retention days.

ClickHouse (`looski_visitors.page_views`, extending `infra/clickhouse/init.sql`) holds pseudonymous events only: workspace, visitor ID, page path, referrer host, timestamp, recipe version. No IP, no user agent, no components. Writes go through a bounded, lossy sender like `diagnostics.rs`. A `TTL` matches the workspace retention setting.

No Redis. Rate limiting and idempotency use the existing Postgres and PGMQ patterns.

### Consent and retention

Each site has a consent mode: `required` (default; the snippet does nothing until the host page calls `visitor.consent()`), or `implied` for jurisdictions where the operator takes that responsibility. The setting is the operator's decision and is recorded in audit. Unresolved visitors and their components are deleted after the retention period (default 90 days). Resolved visitors follow an explicit bounded workspace retention policy; association with a Contact does not imply indefinite retention. A Contact's export includes its visitors and page views; permanent deletion removes the Postgres rows in the same transaction and issues a ClickHouse lightweight delete by visitor ID as a durable job that is retried until confirmed.

### Interfaces

Staff operations share one Rust implementation across HTTP API, CLI and MCP: list and inspect visitors, inspect links and scores, resolve or unlink manually, configure the site, rotate the site key, export. Permissions are `visitors.read`, `visitors.manage` and `visitors.configure` (workspace-only). The Contact timeline composes visitor events only when the viewer holds `visitors.read`. The web UI adds a site setup page with the snippet, a visitors list, and the timeline entries.

## Phases

1. **Foundation.** Migration, site configuration, cookie-only visitor records, page-view events to ClickHouse, consent gating, retention job, export and deletion. No fingerprint yet. Acceptance: a form submission from a cookied browser appears on the Contact timeline with prior page views.
2. **Fingerprint fallback.** Snippet package, component capture, exact and similarity matching, drift-pair logging, manual unlink. Acceptance: clearing cookies and returning within the window re-links at or above threshold; a different machine does not.
3. **Email and concierge resolution.** Signed link tokens in the communications outbox, concierge bridge passes the visitor ID, resolution through Contacts intake. Acceptance: an email click proposes attribution; a forwarded link or scanner cannot verify a Contact or reveal its history.
4. **Operations.** CLI/MCP parity, measured latency for the collection endpoint under throttling, storage growth measurement on ClickHouse per the roadmap note, backup and recovery coverage.

## Tests

Exact and similarity matching including every veto rule; threshold boundaries; Brave-style canvas randomization producing no false link; cookie-present drift pairs recorded; consent mode blocking collection; origin allowlist and throttling; resolution never writes Contact fields; probabilistic inheritance shown with score; contact merge and permanent deletion removing Postgres and ClickHouse data; retention expiry; export completeness; interface parity; no IP or user agent in ClickHouse.

## Open choices

- Recipe weights and the default threshold need measurement on real traffic before the defaults above are treated as final.
- Whether `implied` consent mode ships in the first release or waits for a jurisdiction setting on the workspace.
- Whether the snippet is published from this repository or a separate MIT repository to keep the license boundary unambiguous.

## Implemented first slice

The Suite application, schema 40, staff review/configuration UI, API/CLI/MCP and reusable first-party browser/server adapters are implemented. See [Visitor attribution](../visitors.md) for the actual contract, bounded matching, retention, integration examples and current limits. The browser package is private/unpublished; no collector is mounted on loo.ski by this release. Existing form/booking/concierge/email flows are not automatically wired. Matching is evidence-only and never propagates a contact or grants chat access. Those implementation decisions supersede automatic relinking/inherited resolution in the original proposal.
