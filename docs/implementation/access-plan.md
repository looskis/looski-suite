# Workspace access and module administration plan

Staff administration is shipped and tested in schema 9; see [checkpoint](access-slice.md). Invitations and module selection remain next increments. Continue using the existing role, permission and membership tables rather than a competing access model.

The initial grant authority is an explicit workspace-wide `workspace.access.admin` permission. It deliberately authorizes management of all shipped role grants and member assignments; it is a trusted administrative capability, not a limited team-manager role. Ordinary member/role reads have separate permissions. There is no role-name bypass and no wildcard future action grant. A member lacking this administrative capability cannot modify their own or anyone else's authority. More restricted delegated grant administration requires a later explicit policy, not implicit combinations of ordinary data-edit grants.

Serialize access mutations on the workspace row, check current administrator authority after locking, validate supported action/scope pairs from the permission catalog, use expected versions, and write audit/retry results atomically. Protect at least one active effective access administrator after every role/grant/member change. Removing a membership clears its role assignments, preserving historical ownership references. Restoring a removed member starts with explicitly supplied roles rather than reviving old grants. Scope unions remain per action.

Role definitions can be created, renamed, granted supported actions/scopes and archived. Presets are explicit initial data. No arbitrary permission creation endpoint. Workspace-owned versions and composite foreign keys protect grants and assignments. Staff invitations must separately prove the intended recipient; member administration must not attach an arbitrary Supabase account by an unverified correspondence address.

Optional application selection uses a code-defined catalog and workspace enablement records. An absent enablement row means disabled. Contacts and shared Tasks are foundation capabilities, not selectable CRM dependencies. Every CRM business operation must consult the enabled state; disabling preserves data and pauses queued CRM work. Re-enabling does not implicitly replay old work. Modules should be exposed as available only when their owning operations exist.

Acceptance covers ordinary-member denial, grants with unsupported scopes/actions, cross-workspace role/member IDs, same-action union, final-administrator protection under concurrent changes, removed-member cleanup, current-token revocation through membership state, retries, and CLI/API/MCP/browser parity. Invitations, authentication mail and queued disable/resume require their own connected tests.
