# SaaS market size and valuation: primary-source notes

Research cutoff: 12 October 2026. Dollar figures are US dollars unless stated otherwise. Figures below distinguish analyst forecasts, reported company results, and equity valuations.

## Recommended framing for the video

SaaS represents **hundreds of billions of dollars in annual spending**, while listed cloud software companies represent **trillions of dollars of equity value**. Those are different measurements. A precise current total for a narrowly defined universe of traditional business applications is not established by these sources.

For a current annual-spending hook, say: **“IDC's 2026 forecast implies more than half a trillion dollars in annual SaaS spending.”** Label the implication and the forecast; do not present it as completed 2026 revenue. If using Gartner instead, say **“Gartner forecast roughly $300 billion of SaaS spending for 2025 in its November 2024 outlook.”** These differing scopes and forecast vintages must not be joined into a growth series.

For the stock-market hook, say: **“Bessemer's public cloud index dashboard displayed about $2.8 trillion in combined market capitalization when checked on October 12.”** This is a selected basket, not all global SaaS companies or a SaaS-only enterprise valuation.

IDC's SaaS scope includes security and enterprise applications; its release identifies ERM, security and CRM among SaaS demand categories. Gartner's older table uses its own “cloud application services” taxonomy. Differences may reflect definitions, coverage, forecast revisions and period. The public releases do not provide enough reconciliation detail to allocate the gap among these factors. Do not call $300–500 billion a statistical confidence interval, imply the category doubled in one year, or substitute all software/all cloud for SaaS.

## Company equity value: useful proxy with clear limits

The [BVP Nasdaq Emerging Cloud Index dashboard](https://cloudindex.bvp.com/), retrieved 12 October 2026, displayed:

- Total market cap: **$2.8 trillion**.
- Average revenue multiple: **8.9x**.
- Average revenue growth: **20.6%**.

Treat these as a rounded live-dashboard snapshot, not an audited worldwide industry total or a verified closing valuation at a specified time. The parsed dashboard does not state the precise market-cap valuation timestamp. Its default performance chart yielded zero placeholders; do not use those as actual index returns.

[Nasdaq's official overview](https://indexes.nasdaqomx.com/Index/Overview/EMCLOUD), as retrieved on 12 October, listed **65 components** and described its universe as emerging public cloud software and services companies. Index points are another distinct metric and must not be interpreted as dollars. A selected cohort excludes private companies and can include infrastructure/software services outside the video's narrow traditional application scope. Entire diversified-company market caps cannot be added as if entirely attributable to SaaS.

As a historical valuation-correction reference, [Bessemer's 2023 India SaaS report](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/rise-of-saas-in-india-2023) reported the index's average enterprise-value/next-twelve-month revenue multiple falling from approximately **25x at its high to 6x in the first half of 2023**. This is a historical repricing, not evidence revenue went to zero. Do not compare that forward EV metric directly with the dashboard's unqualified revenue multiple without checking definitions.

For a narrower alternative, the [SaaS Capital Index](https://www.saas-capital.com/the-saas-capital-index/) states data as of 30 September 2026 and covers 63 listed companies. It excludes substantial mixed revenue businesses and many consumer-oriented vendors. Its valuation multiple is **market capitalization divided by annualized latest-quarter GAAP revenue**, with no cash/debt adjustment. That is different from enterprise value divided by forecast revenue. Its image/workbook was not independently extracted here; no numerical median is claimed from secondary summaries.

## Growth and disruption: evidence against literal “going to zero”

[Salesforce's Q2 FY2027 release, 26 August 2026](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx), reports the quarter ended 31 July 2026: revenue **$11.3 billion, up 11% year over year**, and subscription/support revenue **$10.8 billion, up 12%**. These are reported results, not market forecasts. Acquired Informatica contributed $456 million of total revenue and $440 million of subscription/support revenue, so headline growth is not organic growth. One incumbent cannot establish the whole industry's trend, but it directly contradicts an assertion that established SaaS revenue has already collapsed universally.

[Gartner's Australian forecast, 11 May 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-forecasts-australian-organizations-will-spend-more-than-33-billion-on-public-cloud-in-2026), projects SaaS spending of **A$16.377 billion in 2026, up 13.8%**, versus 15% growth in 2025. Its explanation names license optimization, slower seat growth and closer application portfolio scrutiny. This illustrates deceleration plus continued growth in one region; it is not a global growth estimate.

[Gartner, 1 July 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-01-gartner-says-us-dollars-234-billion-in-enterprise-application-software-spend-is-at-risk-from-agentic-artificial-intelligence), estimates **up to $234 billion of enterprise application spending exposed to agentic arbitrage through 2030**, roughly **20% of enterprise application SaaS spending by 2030**. It describes agents performing work across systems and weakening the link between human seats and software revenue. The forecast concerns exposure, not booked losses; “up to” matters. Gartner explicitly expects SaaS to change form rather than disappear. Do not back-calculate a precise present-day market size from its rounded 2030 share.

Editorial inference: the defensible thesis is that AI can reduce the scarcity of application code, change interfaces and seat economics, and redistribute future value among vendors. **“All SaaS is going to zero” is a provocative hypothesis, not a conclusion supported by the market-size or reported-growth evidence.**
